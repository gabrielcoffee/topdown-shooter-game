# CHAMBER 9 — Marketing Plan

Zero-budget, solo-dev, organic-only. Built around one fact from every case study: **the product is 80–90% of the marketing** ([Choo-Choo Charles analysis](https://howtomarketagame.com/2021/10/17/how-choo-choo-charles-earned-wishlists/)). CHAMBER 9's marketable product truth: **"CoD Zombies, but top-down, made by one guy."** Every piece of content leans on that nostalgia — mystery box rolls, wall-buys, nuke/instakill/max-ammo drops, high-round runs. That's the cultural undercurrent we tap, the same way Choo-Choo Charles tapped "evil Thomas the Tank Engine."

## The funnel

Every post exists to move someone one step down:

**clip view → follow → bio link → itch.io page → $1 buy**

One link everywhere: the itch page. No Discord, no mailing list, no Steam page for v1.0 — funnel stays one hop. (Email list is the proven next step when traction shows — see "Later" section.)

## Channels & roles

| Channel | Account | Role | Cadence |
|---|---|---|---|
| TikTok | dev | Discovery engine. Raw gameplay clips. | 4–5 clips/wk |
| YouTube Shorts | dev | Same clips, re-uploaded native. Longer shelf life + search. | same 4–5/wk |
| YouTube (long) | dev | 1 devlog: "I made CoD Zombies top-down in Lua" | 1 every 3–4 wks |
| Instagram Reels | personal | Best 2–3 clips of the week, casual captions | 2–3/wk |
| Instagram Stories | personal | Behind-the-scenes: dev screen, playtest reactions, milestones | whenever, zero pressure |
| LinkedIn | personal | Dev-journey posts, not game ads: "what shipping a game solo taught me about X" | 1/wk max |

Notes:
- 2026 reality: organic reach on TikTok gaming accounts dropped to ~4–8%; YouTube Shorts is the more algorithmically stable channel — so cross-posting is mandatory, not optional ([2026 guide](https://www.game-developers.org/2026-indie-game-production-marketing-guide)).
- Upload native to each platform. Never post a clip with the TikTok watermark to Reels — it gets downranked.
- **Don't post trailers.** Nobody watches trailers as shorts. Post *moments* ([TikTok playbook](https://gamosy.com/blog/tiktok-game-marketing)).

## Content pillars (CHAMBER 9-specific)

Ranked by expected performance:

1. **Mystery box rolls** — slot-machine tension is the single most loop-able moment in the game. "I bet my last 950 points on the box" formats. Gambling reels perform everywhere.
2. **Crowd-clear juice** — nuke wipes, molotov fire patches melting a horde, shotgun pierce through 3 zombies. Satisfying > clever.
3. **Challenge runs** — "knife only to wave 10," "nightmare mode, no wall-buys," high-round attempts. Invites comment-section challenges → reply with a new clip = free content chain.
4. **Before/after polish** — audio off vs on, scenery + wind shader added, muzzle flash added. Devlog-flavored, does well on Shorts and LinkedIn both.
5. **Funny bugs & dev moments** — zombies breakdancing, physics gone wrong. Humanizes the dev account.
6. **"Day X of making my zombie shooter"** — series framing on everything above; the running number itself builds a follow habit.

## Clip format rules

- 15–35 seconds, vertical 9:16 (record 4:3 game at high res, crop center in edit — test that hotbar/HUD survives the crop).
- Hook inside 1.5s: action on frame one, never a logo or title card.
- Text overlay hook on every clip: "CoD Zombies but it's top-down," "I spent 6 months on this $1 game," "wave 23, one bullet left."
- Captions on (sound-off viewers).
- Reply to every comment in the first hour. Comments in hour one drive distribution.
- Same clip everywhere: TikTok + Shorts + Reels. Edit once, post three times.

## Weekly production loop (~3–4h total)

1. One recording session (~1h): play the game with OBS running. **This is also the playtest** — impressions go to PROGRESS.md playtest log. Two birds.
2. One edit session (~1.5h, CapCut): cut 5–7 clips, add text hooks + captions.
3. Schedule the week's posts. Consistent time of day.
4. 15 min/day: reply to comments.

## Launch sequence

Ship date already here — no long pre-launch runway, and that's fine. The Choo-Choo lesson: the announcement *was* the marketing. Ours is the launch.

**Week 0 (this week) — setup + first posts**
- itch.io page: 3–5 GIFs (mystery box roll, nuke wipe, molotov horde), one paragraph, $1. The page is the landing zone — it exists before the first clip goes up.
- Dev accounts created (TikTok + YouTube), bio = one line + itch link.
- First recording session → first 5 clips banked.
- Post clip #1: strongest moment (mystery box or nuke wipe). Not an announcement — a moment.

**Launch week**
- itch release day: "launch clip" on all channels — "I spent X months making CoD Zombies top-down. It's out. $1." with the best 20s of gameplay.
- LinkedIn: the one big post — "I shipped my first game" story, what it taught you, clip embedded. Personal-account framing, not an ad.
- Instagram: story series through launch day (build → ship → live).
- Reddit (free reach, clips already made): r/IndieDev "I made this," r/love2d (LÖVE community loves shipped games), r/WorldWarZ-adjacent zombie subs if rules allow.
- itch: tag properly (action, zombies, top-down shooter), devlog post on release.

**Weeks 2–6 — the grind**
- Hold 4–5 clips/wk. Do not judge anything before ~25 posts; short-form is a lottery where consistency buys tickets.
- Double down on whichever pillar over-performs (check weekly).
- Devlog video #1 on YouTube: "How I made a CoD Zombies game in Lua (solo)" — LÖVE/Lua angle is a real niche hook for the dev audience.
- Micro-outreach: DM/email 10–20 small streamers (1k–10k viewers, zombie/horror/indie niches) with a free key + one-line pitch. Micro beats macro for ROI ([influencer guide](https://iqfluence.io/public/blog/indie-game-influencer-marketing-strategies)); Choo-Choo's creator emailed every target a month out and every one of them played it.

## Weekly scorecard (5 min, one row per week)

| Metric | Where |
|---|---|
| Views + follows per channel | platform analytics |
| Best clip + its pillar | manual note |
| itch page visits | itch dashboard |
| Sales | itch dashboard |

Iterate on pillars, not on effort. Effort stays constant.

## Later (post-v1.0, only if traction)

- **Email list** — itch devlogs + a simple signup; proven path to converting an audience to a bigger launch later ([Zukowski](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/marketing/game-developer-podcast-36-indie-marketing-advice-from-chris-zukowski)).
- **Steam page for the sequel/expanded version** — wishlists only matter on Steam; itch v1.0 is the audience-builder and proof.
- **In-game clip helper** — "save last 10 seconds as GIF" key. Makes content capture free. SEQUEL.md candidate, not v1.0.
